# ₹101, not ₹100: why is there an extra ₹1 in shagun?
Length: 40s | Format: 9:16 | ~102 words VO (English) | Reel type: **Mystery / origin (warm, family)**

## Research summary
- Shagun/gift money is commonly given as ₹11, ₹51, ₹101, ₹501, ₹1001, with an extra ₹1 added.
- **No scriptural or documented origin.** These are customary beliefs (sources agree on these):
  1. **Zero = ending**, and adding 1 makes it a new beginning (amounts ending in 0 considered inauspicious).
  2. **Indivisible odd number**: can't be split equally, so the blessing and the couple stay "undivided".
  3. **A "debt" that keeps the bond going**: the receiving family returns it at the giver's next occasion.
  4. **Metal coin**: the extra ₹1 is traditionally a coin, and metal is considered auspicious.
- Honest framing: it's tradition, not scripture.

Sources: Tribune India / Sentinel Assam ("7 reasons why Indians add one rupee"), Asianet Newsable, Kalinga TV, AstroVed, Hindu Blog.
