# Why are trees (and dividers) painted red & white?
Length: 40s | Format: 9:16 | ~100 words VO (English) | Reel type: **Science / why**

## Research summary
- **Tree trunks:** a coat of white **lime** (calcium hydroxide) plus red **geru** (red ochre), painted in bands up to about 1.5 m (Mumbai BMC practice).
  - Protects against fungal/microbial infection and insects like **stem borers**.
  - White reflects sunlight, keeping bark cooler and preventing **sunscald** cracking (also used on coconut trees in Kerala).
  - Bright bands reflect headlights, so trees are visible to drivers at night.
- **Twist / debate:** some modern arborists argue paint can stop a tree's bark from healing itself.
- **Fun fact:** in 2021 the Sultan of Kedah (Malaysia), visiting Mumbai, asked officials why trunks were painted red and white (Free Press Journal).
- **Road dividers:** in India, divider/island kerbs are usually **black-and-white stripes** (high contrast in day and night); **yellow kerbs = no parking / no stopping**. ⚠ Based on secondary summaries of MoRTH/IRC guidance; wording kept general.
- ⚠ The specific purpose of the red geru isn't clearly sourced, so the script doesn't claim one.

Sources: Free Press Journal (BMC red-white trunks), Onmanorama (lime on coconut trees), Deep Green Permaculture (arborist critique), Bob Vila / Angi (sunscald, visibility), MoRTH road-marking circular 2019, BIS traffic paint standard.
