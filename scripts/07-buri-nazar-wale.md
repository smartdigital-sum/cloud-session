# Lemon, chillies & a shoe: why do trucks carry these?
Length: 40s | Format: 9:16 | ~111 words VO (English) | Reel type: **Mystery / origin (cheeky)**

## Research summary
- **"Buri nazar wale, tera munh kala"** ≈ "Evil-eyed one, may your face be blackened". It's a slogan against *nazar* (the evil eye).
- Related to **"Chashme Baddoor"**, a Persian-derived phrase used across Iran, North India and Pakistan against the evil eye.
- **Shoe** (often a jutti): symbolises throwing a shoe at whoever casts the evil eye.
- **Nimbu-mirchi**: folklore says **Alakshmi** (goddess of misfortune, Lakshmi's opposite) likes sour and spicy food; she eats it and leaves without entering.
- **Practical angle:** citric acid (lemon) and capsaicin (chilli) are known to deter some bugs. ⚠ Whether a hanging string actually works is not proven; keep it as "science fans say".
- Some writers call these superstitions with no religious backing, so the ending lets viewers decide.
- No documented date for when trucks started this.

Sources: Wikipedia (Chashme Baddoor slogan), USC Folklore Archives (lemon-chilli), Deccan Herald, LatestLY, Bharat Express, Hitchhiker's Guide to Religion.
