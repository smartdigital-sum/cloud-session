# Indian head wobble: why do we say "yes" like this?
Length: 40s | Format: 9:16 | ~109 words VO (English) | Reel type: **Mystery / origin** (fun, affectionate)

## Research summary
- Called the "Indian head wobble / bobble". It can mean yes, okay, I understand, thank you, or a polite maybe, depending on context.
- **No documented origin.** These are the popular explanations:
  1. **Politeness / saving face:** a soft response that avoids a blunt "no" (most commonly cited).
  2. **Classical dance:** Bharatanatyam has a side-to-side head movement (often named *Parivahitam*) showing approval/harmony. ⚠ The link to the everyday gesture is a theory, not proven.
  3. **Musical languages:** Indian languages rise and fall in tone, and the head moves along. ⚠ A hypothesis only.
- **Twist (well-sourced):** in Bulgaria, shaking the head side to side means YES and nodding means NO.
- Tone: affectionate and proud ("we all do it"), never mocking. No exaggerated accents or caricatures.

Sources: Wikipedia "Head bobble"; Commisceo Global; Remo "Indian Head Wobble"; Peace Corps "Yes or no depends on which country you're in"; Culture Trip (Bulgaria).
