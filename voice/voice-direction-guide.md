# Voice direction guide

How to get an emotional, human-sounding voiceover for every reel, in English, Hindi and Assamese.

## 1. The golden rules
1. **Talk to one friend, not an audience.** Picture someone sitting across from you. Say "you", not "people".
2. **The first 2 seconds carry the most energy.** Start mid-thought, a little louder and faster, like you just noticed something.
3. **Change speed often.** Go fast through setup lines and slow down for the payoff. A flat pace is what makes a voice sound robotic.
4. **Pause before every reveal.** Take a half-second breath before the answer, the theory name or the twist.
5. **Smile on curious lines.** People can hear a smile. Don't smile on serious or heritage lines.
6. **The ending is an invitation, not a summary.** Use a warm, playful tone, like asking a friend a question.

## 2. Notation used in the scripts
| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `/` | short breath (~0.3s) |
| `//` | full pause (~0.7s), used before a reveal |
| **bold** | stress this word |
| ↑ | pitch goes up (question, surprise) |
| ↓ | pitch drops (final, serious) |
| (fast) / (slow) | change of pace for that line |
| 😮 😏 🙂 😌 🔥 | emotion to feel while saying it |

## 3. Emotion by reel type
| Reel type | Main emotion | Pace | Energy arc | Example topics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mystery / "where did it come from?"** | Curious, playful, detective-like | Medium-fast, slows at reveals | Hook high → steady → each theory a mini peak → playful end | Horn OK Please, nimbu-mirchi |
| **Pride / heritage (Assam)** | Warm, proud, goosebumps | Slower, rich | Soft start → build → peak at the "war cry" or emotional moment → warm end | Joi Aai Axom, Muga silk, Bihu |
| **Science / "why does this happen?"** | Amazed, clear, confident | Medium | Hook surprise → calm explanation → "wow" peak → curious end | Jatinga, Assam floods, earthquakes |
| **Myth-busting** | Slightly cheeky, confident | Fast | Hook challenges the viewer → the myth (mock-serious) → truth (punchy) → smug smile | toothpaste marks, petrol pump trick |

## 4. Recording your own voice (phone is fine)
- Record in a room with curtains, a bed or clothes around you. A wardrobe full of clothes is ideal. Avoid tiled rooms.
- Hold the phone about 15–20 cm from your mouth, slightly to the side so your breath doesn't hit the mic.
- **Stand up** while recording. It gives you more energy.
- Do 3 takes of each scene: one normal, one with more emotion than feels natural, one in between. The "too much" take usually sounds right in a reel.
- Record each scene as a separate clip. That makes it easier to match the timings.
- Drink water, and avoid milk or tea right before recording.

## 6. Language notes
- **English:** an Indian-English accent is good, and it feels local. Don't fake an American accent.
- **Hindi:** use conversational Hindi, and Hinglish words (theory, comment, truck) are fine. Avoid shuddh (formal) Hindi like a news anchor. Say "बताइए" warmly, not formally.
- **Assamese:** a warm, homely tone, like an elder sibling telling a story. Respectful words like আপুনি are good, but don't sound stiff. For heritage topics, the emotion should feel personal ("our Assam").

## 7. Checklist before export
- [ ] The hook (0–2s) sounds like it was caught mid-thought, not "Hello friends".
- [ ] There's at least one clear pause before each reveal.
- [ ] The speed changes at least 3 times.
- [ ] The final line sounds like a question to a friend.
- [ ] Each language's track fits the scene timings.
