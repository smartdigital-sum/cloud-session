## English

**Style instructions**
```
You are a calm, deep-voiced, wise mentor narrating a 60-second vertical reel called "You're not lazy, you're avoiding a feeling". Speak directly to one listener ("you"), like a trusted older friend. Indian English accent. About 140 words per minute, with short deliberate pauses between lines. Open firm and calm, add dry humour on the list of avoidance tasks, explain the research clearly, go serious on the cost, empathetic on guilt, warm and encouraging on the fix. End friendly, with a smile on the question.
```

**Text**
```
[calm] You're not lazy. You're avoiding a feeling.
[knowingly] You'll clean your room, reply to old messages, reorganise your phone… anything except the one task. Lazy people don't work that hard.
[curious] Researchers call it short-term mood repair. The task brings boredom or self-doubt. You switch tabs. Instant relief.
[serious] But future you pays. In a 1997 study, student procrastinators felt great early on… then ended the term more stressed, sicker, with lower grades.
[gently] And guilt makes it worse. In a 2010 study, students who forgave themselves after procrastinating on one exam procrastinated less on the next.
[warmly] So forgive yesterday. Then shrink the task: not "write the report", just "open the file".
[friendly] Next time, don't ask "Why am I lazy?" Ask "What am I feeling?" What task are you avoiding right now? Tell me below.
```

[gently] और गिल्ट इसे और बिगाड़ता है। दो हज़ार दस की एक स्टडी में, जिन स्टूडेंट्स ने एक परीक्षा पर टालने के बाद ख़ुद को माफ़ किया, उन्होंने अगली पर कम टाला।
[warmly] तो कल को माफ़ कीजिए। फिर काम छोटा कीजिए: "रिपोर्ट लिखो" नहीं, बस "फ़ाइल खोलो।"
[friendly] अगली बार मत पूछिए, "मैं आलसी क्यों हूँ?" पूछिए, "मैं क्या महसूस कर रहा हूँ?" आप अभी किस काम से बच रहे हैं? नीचे बताइए।
