## HOOK (0:00–0:15)

**[NARRATION]**
"Hurry." "Just one small favour." "Everyone's doing it."
(pause) If you've heard all three this week, someone may be working on you.

**[NARRATION]**
In this series, we've taken apart nine tactics. They look different, but they share one goal: to get you to decide before you think, or to stop trusting yourself.
So here are seven signs you're being manipulated, and one simple defence for each.
(pause) And at the end, why just knowing them makes you harder to fool.

**[VISUAL]** Nine episode thumbnails from the series slide into a grid, then fold into a single shield. Title card: **7 SIGNS**, with a checklist of seven empty boxes on the shield.

## MAIN BODY

### Beat 1: Signs 1–2: rushed, and the growing yes (0:40–1:15)

**[NARRATION]**
Sign one: you're being rushed. "Your account will be blocked." "This offer ends tonight." Urgency shrinks your thinking time on purpose.
Defence: pause. Anything real will still be real in ten minutes.
Sign two: small yeses keep growing. A tiny favour, then a bigger one, then bigger still.
Defence: judge each request on its own. "If this were the first thing they asked, would I agree?"
(pause) Speed and steps. Both are designed to stop you looking up.

**[VISUAL]** Box 1 ticks: a countdown timer melts when a hand presses PAUSE. Box 2 ticks: a staircase of requests (₹1 → ₹500 → ₹50,000); the figure stops and looks at one step under a spotlight. Corner badges: "VIDEO 01", "VIDEO 02".

_Tone: brisk, clear; firm last line._

### Beat 2: Signs 3–4: the big ask, and the gift (1:15–1:50)

**[NARRATION]**
Sign three: a huge request, then a "reasonable" one. The first was built to be refused, so the second feels like a compromise.
Defence: forget the first ask. Judge the second alone.
Sign four: a gift, then a request, close together. A free sample, a favour, a "free prize" that needs a small fee.
Defence: you can say "thank you" and still say "no". And never pay to receive something free.
(pause) A no doesn't create a debt. Neither does a gift you didn't ask for.

**[VISUAL]** Box 3 ticks: a slammed door reopens with a small box; an eraser wipes the first request off a whiteboard. Box 4 ticks: a gift box with a hidden hook; a "PAY ₹99 FOR FREE GIFT" message gets a ✗. Badges: "VIDEO 03", "VIDEO 04".

_Tone: knowing, steady; firm last line._

### Beat 3: Signs 5–6: the costume, and the crowd (1:50–2:25)

**[NARRATION]**
Sign five: a title, a uniform or a logo you can't check. A "police officer" on a video call. A "bank head office" caller.
Defence: verify through a second channel. Hang up, find the official number yourself, and call back.
Sign six: "everyone's doing it". Reviews, followers, a group full of people posting profits.
Defence: don't count the crowd, check it. Read the worst reviews, and be wary of any group where nobody asks questions.
(pause) Authority and crowds are useful. Fakes of both are cheap.

**[VISUAL]** Box 5 ticks: a video-call "officer" is revealed against a printed backdrop; the figure dials a number from an official site. Box 6 ticks: a wall of five-star reviews; copied ones light up red; one person with twenty phones. Badges: "VIDEO 05", "VIDEO 06".

_Tone: serious, protective; firm last line._

### Beat 4: Sign 7: you stop trusting yourself (2:25–3:00)

**[NARRATION]**
Sign seven is the quietest, and the most important: you start doubting yourself.
You're told things never happened. Affection comes fast, then turns cold when you set a boundary. You raise a problem and end up apologising. And slowly, you see your friends less.
Defence: keep a private record, slow things down, and talk to someone you trust outside the situation.
(pause) Manipulation works best in isolation. Connection is the antidote.

**[VISUAL]** Box 7 ticks slowly. A dimming gas lamp, a heart balloon tied to a padlock, a BLAME arrow spinning back: each one fades in, then out. The figure opens a notebook, then walks out into daylight to meet a friend. Badges: "VIDEO 07", "VIDEO 08", "VIDEO 09".

_Tone: gentle, serious; warm last line._

## PAYOFF / TWIST (3:00–3:30)

**[NARRATION]**
And here's the twist: you're already harder to fool. In 2022, researchers led by Jon Roozenbeek found that short videos explaining manipulation techniques helped people spot them, even in a real test on YouTube. Psychologists call it "prebunking".
So remember three habits: pause, verify, and tell someone.
(pause) Manipulation needs speed, silence and secrecy. You now have time, questions, and people.

**[VISUAL]** A shield forms over the figure as the seven ticks glow. A YouTube-style player shows a short explainer; a "PREBUNKING" label stamps across it. Caption: _Roozenbeek et al., 2022, Science Advances_. Three big words fill the screen: **PAUSE · VERIFY · TELL SOMEONE**. Helplines small at the bottom: **1930 · 112 · 181 · Tele-MANAS 14416**.

_Tone: warm, empowering (peak); slow, firm last line._
