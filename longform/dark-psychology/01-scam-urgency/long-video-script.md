## HOOK (0:00–0:15)

**[NARRATION]**
"Your bank account will be blocked in ten minutes. Share the OTP now."
(pause) Your heart races. And that racing heart is exactly what the scammer wants.

**[VISUAL]** A phone lights up in a dark room with a red SMS banner and a countdown "09:59". A hand trembles over the keypad. Close-up on a heartbeat line spiking on the screen's reflection.

_Tone: urgent read of the message, then a slow, low reveal._

## SETUP (0:15–0:40)

**[NARRATION]**
Have you ever noticed that almost every scam message is in a hurry? "Last chance." "Act now." "Police case against you."
That isn't bad writing. It's the weapon. Fear and urgency switch off the part of you that would normally stop and check.
(pause) This video is about spotting that switch, before anyone flips it.

**[VISUAL]** A wall of scam messages scrolls past, each with a red clock icon. A light switch labelled CAREFUL THINKING sits on a wall; a shadowy hand reaches toward it, and freezes.

_Tone: curious, protective; firm last line._

## MAIN BODY

### Beat 1: How urgency hijacks your brain (0:40–1:15)

**[NARRATION]**
Researchers at the University of Cambridge studied the tricks behind real scams, and listed seven principles that con artists exploit. One of them is time.
When you're rushed, you stop weighing evidence and start reacting. Fear narrows attention: you think about the threat, not about whether the threat is real.
(pause) A scammer doesn't need you to believe everything. Just to act before you think.

**[VISUAL]** A list of seven icons; the clock icon lights up: TIME. A wide-angle view of a room narrows into a tunnel focused on a single red "BLOCKED" word, while the clues at the edges (a strange number, spelling mistakes) blur out. Caption: _Stajano & Wilson, 2011, University of Cambridge_.

_Tone: thoughtful, clear; pointed last line._

### Beat 2: The scams you'll recognise (1:15–1:50)

**[NARRATION]**
In India, you'll recognise the patterns. A "KYC update" that must happen today. A courier "with drugs in your name". A "customs officer" on WhatsApp. Or a "prize" that expires at midnight.
And the newest: the so-called "digital arrest", where fake police or CBI officers keep you on a video call for hours, isolated and terrified, until you transfer money. And it doesn't matter how educated you are: doctors, engineers and retired officers have lost savings this way.
(pause) Every one of them has the same clock ticking in the background.

**[VISUAL]** Quick cards: KYC UPDATE TODAY · PARCEL SEIZED · CUSTOMS CALL · PRIZE EXPIRES 12:00. Then a video-call screen showing a fake uniform and badge (clearly stylised, no real insignia); the caller's background is a cardboard set. A small clock ticks in the corner of every card.

_Tone: serious, recognisable._

### Beat 3: The official facts (1:50–2:25)

**[NARRATION]**
So here are the facts, straight from official advisories. The Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre says the CBI, police, customs, ED and judges do not arrest people over video calls.
Prime Minister Modi also warned in Mann Ki Baat: there is no such thing as "digital arrest" in Indian law.
And banks don't ask for your OTP or PIN. Ever. If anyone in a uniform demands money on a video call, that alone tells you it's fake.

**[VISUAL]** Three bold fact cards stamped ✓ OFFICIAL: "NO ARRESTS BY VIDEO CALL" · "NO 'DIGITAL ARREST' IN LAW" · "BANKS NEVER ASK FOR OTP / PIN". A calm on-screen note: _Source: I4C advisory, Oct 2024_.

_Tone: firm, reassuring, authoritative._

### Beat 4: The second trick, isolation (2:25–3:00)

**[NARRATION]**
Notice one more trick: isolation. Scammers say, "Don't tell anyone, it's confidential." "Stay on the call."
Because the moment you talk to someone calm, a family member, a friend, a bank branch, the spell breaks.
(pause) Urgency stops you from thinking. Isolation stops anyone else from thinking for you.

**[VISUAL]** The victim-figure sits alone inside a glass box labelled "CONFIDENTIAL", the call still running. Outside, family and a bank counter are blurred. A hand taps on the glass; the box cracks and light pours in.

_Tone: knowing, quietly urgent; weighty last line._

## PAYOFF / TWIST (3:00–3:30)

**[NARRATION]**
So here's the one habit that beats almost all of them: slow down, and verify through a second channel.
Hang up. Call your bank on the number printed on your card, not the number they gave you. Tell someone you trust.
If you've been cheated, report it immediately on 1930, the national cyber-crime helpline, or at cybercrime.gov.in. Speed matters there, not on the scammer's clock.
(pause) Real officials can wait for you to check. Scammers can't.

**[VISUAL]** Three steps animate: ✋ HANG UP → 📞 CALL THE NUMBER ON YOUR CARD → 👨‍👩‍👧 TELL SOMEONE. Then a large, clear on-screen panel: **1930 · cybercrime.gov.in**. The countdown clock from the hook freezes and fades.

_Tone: warm, empowering (peak); calm, confident last line._
