# 03 · Reel: Big No → Small Yes

Short version of [long-video-script.md](long-video-script.md) for YouTube Shorts / Instagram Reels.
**Format:** 9:16 vertical · **Length:** ~57s · **Words:** ~105 English (~135 wpm with pauses)
**Voiceover:** English, Hindi, Assamese → [short-reel-voice.md](short-reel-voice.md) (same voice as the series: Charon)

Rules: hook on screen + voice in the first 2 seconds, no intro or logo. One idea per scene. The last line asks a question, to get comments and loop viewers back to the start.

## Scene table

| # | Time | Visual (9:16) | On-screen text | Voiceover (English) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:03 | A door slams ("NO"), then reopens a crack with a small box | **BIG NO → SMALL YES** | They asked for something huge, so you'd say yes to this. |
| 2 | 0:03–0:12 | A "2 YEARS × 2 HRS/WEEK" card gets a big ✗ | 1975 · ALMOST ALL REFUSED | In 1975, students were asked to counsel young offenders for two years. Almost all refused. |
| 3 | 0:12–0:22 | A zoo-ticket card: half the hands go up; a second crowd: 1 in 6 | ~50% vs ~1 IN 6 | Then: "Just one zoo trip?" About half agreed. Asked only about the zoo: about one in six. |
| 4 | 0:22–0:31 | On a bridge, one figure steps back; the other feels pulled forward | THEIR "STEP BACK" PULLS YOU | Why? Their "step back" feels like a concession, and you feel you should meet them halfway. |
| 5 | 0:31–0:40 | Bazaar: "₹2,000!" → "OK, ₹800, final." | THE ABSURD FIRST PRICE | You'll see it in bargaining: an absurd first price makes the "final price" feel generous. |
| 6 | 0:40–0:50 | An eraser wipes the first request off a whiteboard | JUDGE THE SECOND ALONE | Your defence: forget the first request. "If this were the only ask, would I say yes?" |
| 7 | 0:50–0:57 | A wall of price tags | WILDEST FIRST PRICE? 👇 | What's the wildest first price you've been quoted? Tell me below. |

**Loop:** scene 7's price tags cut straight back to the door slamming in scene 1.
