## SETUP (0:15–0:40)

**[NARRATION]**
Have you ever been swept off your feet so fast it was almost dizzying? Sometimes, that's just real chemistry.
But sometimes, overwhelming affection early on is used as a tool. It's called love bombing.
(pause) So how do you tell the difference? That's the hard part, and the honest answer matters.

**[VISUAL]** A figure spins in a whirl of hearts, dizzy and smiling. The hearts slowly arrange themselves into a net. Title card: **LOVE BOMBING**, with a heart-shaped balloon whose string is tied to a small padlock.

_Tone: curious, warm; thoughtful last line._

## MAIN BODY

### Beat 1: Where the term came from (0:40–1:15)

**[NARRATION]**
The phrase was first linked to some religious groups in the 1970s. Newcomers were surrounded by warmth, praise and attention from day one.
It felt like belonging. But critics said the affection came with strings: once people were attached, the warmth could be pulled back to keep them in line.
Today, the term is used mostly about relationships, and especially online.
(pause) The affection wasn't the problem. The strings were.

**[VISUAL]** A 1970s-style hall: a newcomer at the door is swept into a circle of smiling people with flowers. Golden threads run from each smile to the newcomer's wrists. Later, the circle turns its back, and the threads tighten. Transition to a modern phone with a dating app.

_Tone: intrigued storyteller; quiet last line._

### Beat 2: What research says, and doesn't (1:15–1:50)

**[NARRATION]**
Here's the honest part: research on love bombing is limited. One of the few studies, from 2017, surveyed 484 college students in the United States.
People who reported more love-bombing behaviour tended to score higher on narcissistic traits and lower on self-esteem, and they texted and used social media more in their relationships.
But that's one survey, of students, and it shows a link, not a cause.
(pause) So treat any confident checklist online with care. Including this one.

**[VISUAL]** A single slim journal on a mostly empty shelf labelled LOVE BOMBING RESEARCH. A survey sheet with "484 STUDENTS". Two dots joined by a dotted line labelled LINK, with a crossed-out arrow labelled CAUSE. A viral "10 SIGNS" listicle gets a small "handle with care" sticker.

_Tone: honest, balanced; wry last line._

### Beat 3: Signs worth noticing (1:50–2:25)

**[NARRATION]**
Still, some patterns are worth noticing. Intensity that runs far ahead of how well you actually know each other. Pressure to commit quickly: "We're meant to be. Why wait?"
Gifts and attention that leave you feeling you owe them. And a sudden switch to coldness or anger when you set a boundary, or spend time with others.
Romance scams often follow this script online: weeks of devotion, then an emergency, or an "investment" you can't miss.
(pause) Real affection can handle "let's slow down". Control can't.

**[VISUAL]** A speedometer labelled INTENSITY racing past a much slower one labelled HOW WELL YOU KNOW THEM. A gift box with an I OWE YOU tag. A warm face that turns icy when the figure says "I'm meeting friends tonight". A chat: weeks of hearts, then "Emergency! Can you send ₹50,000?" and "Invest with me, guaranteed returns".

_Tone: thoughtful, serious; firm last line._

### Beat 4: Fast doesn't always mean fake (2:25–3:00)

**[NARRATION]**
To be fair, some people really do fall hard and fast, and it works out. Excitement alone isn't a red flag.
The difference often shows over time. Does their kindness stay when you say no, see your friends, or ask for space? Or does it turn into guilt, sulking or pressure?
(pause) The test isn't how fast it starts. It's what happens when you hit pause.

**[VISUAL]** An older couple laughing over a photo from a whirlwind first week. Then a split path: one partner says "Of course, see your friends!"; the other folds their arms with a storm cloud overhead. A big PAUSE button pressed.

_Tone: fair, warm; clear last line._

## PAYOFF / TWIST (3:00–3:30)

**[NARRATION]**
So here's your defence. Slow it down on purpose, and watch the reaction. Keep your friends and family close; love bombing works best when you're isolated.
Never send money to someone you've only met online, however real it feels.
And if a relationship is making you feel unsafe, talk to someone. In India: 112, women's helpline 181, Tele-MANAS 14416. For online fraud: 1930.
(pause) Love that's real will still be there next week.

**[VISUAL]** The figure turns down the speedometer and invites friends over. A "SEND MONEY?" button greys out. On screen, clearly: **112 · 181 · Tele-MANAS 14416 · Cyber fraud 1930**. The heart balloon from the setup floats free; the padlock falls away.

_Tone: warm, empowering (peak); soft, firm last line._
