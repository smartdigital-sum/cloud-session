# 05 · Reel: Silent Doesn't Mean Harmless

Short version of [long-video-script.md](long-video-script.md) for YouTube Shorts / Instagram Reels, written with the *Short-Form Storytelling* guide: Hook → Lock-In → Body → Rehook → Body → Rehook → Body → Closing → CTA.
**Format:** 9:16 vertical · **Length:** ~60s · **Words:** ~150 English (~150 wpm, short pauses only)
**Voiceover:** English, Hindi, Assamese → [short-reel-voice.md](short-reel-voice.md) (same voice as the series: Charon)

## What changed and why

| Problem before | Fix (guide rule) |
|---|---|
| Opened whispered and hedged ("It may still be distracting you"). | **Hook:** subject + stakes in the first line, said firmly. |
| No reason to stay past the first study. | **Lock-in:** "the worst spot is the one most of us pick". |
| "Later studies are mixed" stopped the momentum. | Honest line kept, but it now **rehooks** straight into the next pull ("this next one you've felt yourself"). |
| One flat CTA. | **CTA:** full video, then the comment question that loops to the start. |

## Scene table

| # | Part | Time | Visual (9:16) | On-screen text | Voiceover (English) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 0:00–0:04 | A hand slaps the phone face down (thud); mute icon; a glowing thread snaps from phone to head | **FACE DOWN. ON SILENT.** | Face down. On silent. Your phone is still stealing your focus. |
| 2 | Lock-in | 0:04–0:10 | Three spots light up: desk · bag · other room; a "?" hovers over the desk | **THE WORST SPOT?** | Scientists tested where people keep their phones. And the worst spot is the one most of us pick. |
| 3 | Body 1 | 0:10–0:20 | Three desks with brainpower bars; other room fullest, face-down desk lowest | OTHER ROOM WINS · 2017 | In 2017, people took memory tests with their phones on silent. Phone in another room? Best scores. Face down on the desk? Worst. |
| 4 | Rehook 1 | 0:20–0:25 | Bars wobble, shrink a little; cut to the viewer's own desk | SMALLER… BUT | Later labs found that effect smaller, but this next one you've felt yourself. |
| 5 | Body 2 | 0:25–0:37 | Phone lights up, untouched; "WHO WAS THAT?" bubble; next taps turn red | THE BUZZ YOU IGNORE STILL COSTS YOU | In 2015, people got a call or text in the middle of a focus task. They didn't even touch the phone. They still made more mistakes, because one buzz makes your brain ask: who was that? |
| 6 | Rehook 2 | 0:37–0:41 | Pocket glows… then nothing | IT GETS STRANGER | And then it gets stranger: your brain starts inventing buzzes. |
| 7 | Body 3 | 0:41–0:47 | Figure pats an empty pocket; 9 of 10 student icons light up | 9 IN 10 FELT FAKE BUZZES | In one study, nearly nine in ten students felt their phone vibrate when it hadn't. |
| 8 | Closing | 0:47–0:54 | Phone carried to another room, door shuts; the thread snaps | DISTANCE > WILLPOWER | So don't fight it with willpower. For your most important hour, put the phone in another room. |
| 9 | CTA | 0:54–1:00 | Empty desk; question pops up, then cut to scene 1 | WHERE'S YOUR PHONE? 📱 | Full breakdown's linked below. So, where's your phone right now? |

**Loop:** the question lands on the desk, which cuts straight back to the hand slapping the phone face down in scene 1.
