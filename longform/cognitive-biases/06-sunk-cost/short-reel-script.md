# 06 · Reel: Why Do You Stay?

Short version of [long-video-script.md](long-video-script.md) for YouTube Shorts / Instagram Reels.
**Format:** 9:16 vertical · **Length:** ~57s · **Words:** ~110 English (~135 wpm with pauses)
**Voiceover:** English, Hindi, Assamese → [short-reel-voice.md](short-reel-voice.md) (same voice as the series: Charon)

Rules: hook on screen + voice in the first 2 seconds, no intro or logo. One idea per scene. The last line asks a question, to get comments and loop viewers back to the start.

## Scene table

| # | Time | Visual (9:16) | On-screen text | Voiceover (English) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:03 | A bored viewer slumps in a cinema seat, eyes on the EXIT sign | **WHY DO YOU STAY?** | Why do you finish movies you hate? |
| 2 | 0:03–0:12 | A heavy ALREADY SPENT backpack tilts the figure forward | SUNK COST FALLACY | Because you already paid. It's called the sunk cost fallacy: money or time that's gone, steering what you do next. |
| 3 | 0:12–0:23 | Three ticket stubs (full / small discount / big discount); attendance bars, full price highest | 1982 THEATRE TEST 🎭 | In a 1982 theatre study, people randomly charged full price for season tickets went to more plays than people given discounts. |
| 4 | 0:23–0:31 | Receipt turns red: LOSS; an overloaded buffet plate | "PAISA VASOOL" 🍛 | Quitting turns a cost into a loss, and losses feel louder. Hello, "paisa vasool" buffet plate. |
| 5 | 0:31–0:39 | A white supersonic jet; banknotes stream into its engines | THE CONCORDE FALLACY ✈️ | Even governments fall for it. It's also called the Concorde fallacy, after the supersonic jet. |
| 6 | 0:39–0:51 | A blank page "TODAY"; the backpack drops away | THE FRESH-START QUESTION | The fix: ask, "If I started fresh today, would I choose this?" If not, leaving doesn't waste it. Staying adds to it. |
| 7 | 0:51–0:57 | A half-read book and a half-watched series | WHAT DID YOU FINISH? 👇 | What did you finish only because you'd started? Tell me below. |

**Loop:** scene 7's half-watched series cuts straight back to the cinema seat in scene 1.
