Have you ever noticed that losing ₹100 can ruin your day, but finding ₹100 barely makes it?
That feeling has a name: loss aversion. It's one of the most famous ideas in behavioural economics, and it helped win a Nobel Prize.
(pause) But lately, some scientists have asked: is it as strong as we thought?

## MAIN BODY

### Beat 1: Prospect theory, losses loom larger (0:40–1:15)

**[NARRATION]**
In 1979, Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky published prospect theory. One of its central ideas: losses loom larger than gains.
In their later estimates, a loss hurt roughly twice as much as an equal gain felt good.
That's why many people turn down a fair coin flip unless the possible win is about double the possible loss.
(pause) Your brain keeps two accounts. And the "loss" account is louder.

**[VISUAL]** A simple S-shaped value curve draws itself: gentle slope for gains, steep drop for losses (no numbers on the axes). Two ledgers open: GAINS (soft voice bubble) and LOSSES (loud megaphone). Caption: _Kahneman & Tversky, 1979; Tversky & Kahneman, 1992_.

_Tone: thoughtful, clear; knowing on the last line._

### Beat 2: The coffee mug experiment (1:15–1:50)

**[NARRATION]**
Then came a famous experiment with coffee mugs. In 1990, economists gave half of a group of students a university mug. Then they let them trade.
The mug owners asked for roughly twice as much, or more, to sell it as the others were willing to pay to buy it.
Same mug. Minutes of ownership.
(pause) Once something is yours, giving it up feels like a loss. It's called the endowment effect.

**[VISUAL]** A lecture hall; mugs with a university crest appear on half the desks. Owners hug their mugs; non-owners hold small price tags. A seesaw: SELL PRICE (about double) vs BUY PRICE. Caption: _Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler, 1990_.

_Tone: intrigued; amused on "Minutes of ownership."_

### Beat 3: "Don't miss out!" (1:50–2:25)

**[NARRATION]**
You'll see it everywhere once you look. "Don't miss out!" sale timers. Free trials that feel hard to cancel, because losing access feels worse than never having it.
Investors holding falling shares, waiting to "get back to even" rather than accept the loss. Even an app's "Your streak will be lost!" message plays the same trick.
(pause) Marketers often frame offers as something you're about to lose.

**[VISUAL]** A countdown banner: "ONLY 02:59 LEFT!". A streaming app's "Cancel?" screen with a sad face. A share-price chart sliding down while a hand clutches the line. Final: a small "LOSS FRAME" label sticks onto each one.

_Tone: knowing, slightly wry._

### Beat 4: The twist, smaller than we thought? (2:25–3:00)

**[NARRATION]**
But here's the twist. In 2018, psychologists David Gal and Derek Rucker argued that loss aversion isn't a general law of the mind. Many classic results, they said, can be explained in other ways, like simply preferring to do nothing.
Then in 2024, a huge review pooled more than six hundred estimates from 150 studies. The average? Losses weighed about 1.96 times as much as gains.
(pause) So loss aversion seems real, but it depends on the situation, and it's a little smaller than the famous number.

**[VISUAL]** The loud LOSSES megaphone gets a "?" sticker; two scholar silhouettes argue across a table (no real faces). Then a big pile of 607 small cards pours into a funnel; out comes a single dial pointing at ~2× with a shaded range around it. Captions: _Gal & Rucker, 2018_ · _Brown, Imai, Vieider & Camerer, 2024_.

_Tone: serious, balanced; careful on the conclusion._

## PAYOFF / TWIST (3:00–3:30)

**[NARRATION]**
So how do you protect yourself? When a choice feels scary, flip the frame.
Ask: "If I didn't already own this, would I buy it today?" Ask: "Am I avoiding a loss, or just avoiding a feeling?"
And look at the whole picture: one small loss inside many good bets is normal.
(pause) Losses feel louder. That doesn't make them bigger.

**[VISUAL]** A picture frame flips from LOSE to CHOOSE. The viewer-figure looks at an old subscription and a falling share with a calm "Would I buy this today?" thought bubble. A grid of 10 coin flips: mostly green, one red; the red one shrinks to its true size.

_Tone: warm, empowering (peak); slow last line._
