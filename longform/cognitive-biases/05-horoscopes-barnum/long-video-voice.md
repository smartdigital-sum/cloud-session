[curious] Have you ever read a horoscope or a personality quiz and thought: "How does it know me so well?"

[calm] In this video: a 1948 classroom experiment, where its magic words really came from, and the quizzes using them today.
[short pause] [firmly] And a five-second test for any reading.

[intrigued] In 1948, psychologist Bertram Forer gave his students a personality test.
A week later, each student received a "personal" description, written just for them, supposedly.
Then he asked: how accurate is this, from zero to five? The average rating was about four point three. Almost perfect.
[short pause] Then he revealed the secret. Every student had received exactly the same text.

[mysterious] And where did that text come from? Forer had put it together largely from statements in a newsstand astrology book.
Lines that sound personal, but fit almost anyone: "You have a great deal of unused capacity." "You prefer a certain amount of change and variety."

[short pause] The Barnum effect.
Not a lie. Not one person's trick. Just words wide enough for anyone to step into.

[curious] So why does a sentence written for everyone feel written for you? [short pause] The answer is in your own memory.

[thoughtful] Why does it work so well? Three reasons.
First, the statements are two-sided: "sometimes outgoing, sometimes reserved" covers nearly everybody.
Second, they're mostly flattering, and we like believing nice things about ourselves.
Third, we're told it's about us, so we search our memories for proof, and we always find some.
[short pause] The reading doesn't describe you. You fill it in.

[knowingly] And it didn't stay in 1948. [short pause] It's on your phone right now.

[knowingly] And it's not just horoscopes. Viral personality quizzes. "Only true introverts will understand" posts. Even some personality reports that sound scientific can lean on vague, flattering lines.

[short pause] A good-sounding description can feel more "you" than the truth.

[warmly] So here's a simple test for any reading, quiz or profile. Ask: "Would this fit my friend too? My cousin? Almost anyone?"
If yes, it's describing humans, not you. And notice how much you're filling in yourself.
Try it with a friend: swap your horoscopes for a week, without telling each other. See if either of you notices.
[long pause] Enjoy it if you like. Just don't let a vague sentence make big decisions for you.
