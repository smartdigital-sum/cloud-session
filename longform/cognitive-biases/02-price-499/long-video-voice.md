[curious] Have you ever noticed that almost nothing in a shop costs a round number? Ninety-nine. Four ninety-nine. Nineteen thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine.

[short pause] And it works on experts too.

[thoughtful] We read numbers left to right. And the brain gives the first digit far more weight than the rest.
By the time you reach the nines, your mind has already filed the price under "four hundred".
Researchers call this the left-digit effect. In lab studies, a price feels noticeably smaller when its first digit drops, like two ninety-nine versus three, but not when only the later digits change.
Quick test: which feels bigger, the gap between two ninety-nine and three hundred, or between three hundred and one and three hundred and two? Same one rupee. But the first one feels like a jump.

[playfully] Shops discovered this long before scientists measured it.
In field experiments with a US clothing catalogue, economists Eric Anderson and Duncan Simester found that prices ending in nine lifted sales. Today, look at almost any sale banner in India, and you'll see the same nines.
In one test, a dress sold more at thirty-nine dollars than at a cheaper thirty-four.
[short pause] A higher price, and more buyers.

[serious] And it's not just shoppers in a hurry.
In 2012, economists studied over 22 million used cars sold at wholesale auctions in the US, where many buyers are professional dealers.
Prices dropped sharply every time the odometer crossed a 10,000-mile mark. A car at 79,900 miles sold for noticeably more than one at 80,100. These were people who buy cars for a living, with time to inspect every vehicle. And still, the first digit pulled the price.
[short pause] Two hundred miles. A big price gap. Because the first digit changed.

[thoughtful] But nines aren't magic everywhere. A price ending in nine can also whisper "discount", which is why many luxury shops prefer clean, round numbers.
And how strong the effect is depends on the product and the shopper. Some studies even suggest round prices feel "right" when we buy with our heart, like a gift, while precise prices feel smarter when we buy with our head.
[short pause] Still, when someone wants you to feel a price is smaller, watch the first digit.

[warmly] So here's your defence. Round up, in your head, every time. Four ninety-nine is five hundred. Nineteen thousand nine ninety-nine is twenty thousand. Ninety-nine rupees a month is about twelve hundred a year.
And compare by unit, not by sticker.
[long pause] The shop chose that number for your brain. You can choose to read it properly.

[friendly] Tell me in the comments: what's the silliest "ninety-nine" price you've ever seen?
And subscribe. Next: why the first number you hear can quietly control your choices, even when it's obviously random.
