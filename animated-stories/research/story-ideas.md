# Story ideas: short Blender animations for India
Every idea is an original story built on a proven Indian story type (see audience-research.md). Runtime is 1:30–2:30, vertical 9:16.
"Build" counts what you'd have to model: characters (C) and sets (S). Fewer is faster.

Scores are 1–5 (5 = best). "Ease" means ease in Blender for a solo creator.

## 1. Khanak (The Clink) ★ first film
**Type:** emotional family · **Build:** 3C (one only in a single shot), 3S (home, bazaar, school gate) · **Length:** 2:18
**Logline:** An 8-year-old saves every coin in his clay gullak for a red cricket bat, while his grandmother squints through cracked, taped glasses and keeps saying "चल जाएगा" (it'll do). When he finally smashes the gullak to buy her new glasses instead, he finds far more coins than he saved, and a shaky note: "for Chhotu's bat — Dadi."
**Why it works:** the gullak, the pallu knot full of coins and the phrase "chal jayega" are deeply Indian and nostalgic. Both characters sacrifice for each other, and the ending invites "tag your dadi/nani" comments. The story is told through sound (clinks), so it needs very little dialogue.

## 2. Bhootu ★ series
**Type:** horror-comedy · **Build:** 2C (reuses Dadi), 1S (reuses the home set) · **Length:** 60–90s per episode
**Logline:** A small, sheet-like ghost moves into Dadi's house to haunt it, but Dadi isn't scared at all. She feeds him, oils his "hair", makes him do Chhotu's homework, and drags him to the temple fair. Every episode he tries a new scare and ends up becoming part of the family.
**Episode hooks:** "Bhootu tries the upside-down-on-the-ceiling trick" · "Bhootu vs. Dadi's chappal" · "Bhootu is afraid of the pressure-cooker whistle" · "Bhootu's first Diwali".
**Why it works:** folklore horror-comedy is India's hottest genre (Stree, Munjya). A ghost with no legs or face rig is the easiest character you will ever animate, and the format can grow into a series.

## 3. Tedha Diya (The Crooked Lamp) ★ Diwali special
**Type:** festival · **Build:** objects as characters, 2S (potter's stall, a home doorstep) · **Length:** 1:30
**Logline:** At a potter's stall, one lopsided clay diya is passed over by every buyer while the perfect ones sell. On Diwali night the power goes out, the fancy electric lights die and the wind blows out the perfect diyas. Only the crooked one, sheltered by its tilt, keeps burning and lights a little girl's doorway.
**Why it works:** handmade versus machine-made (support your local potter), "your flaws are your strength", and a festival spike every year. Diyas with tiny eyes need no human rig, and flame plus warm light looks beautiful in EEVEE.
**Timing:** Diwali 2026 is on 8 Nov, which is too soon. Make it for Diwali 2027 and re-post it every year.

## 4. Sudama ki Potli (Sudama's Bundle)
**Type:** mythology / devotional · **Build:** 2C + royal and village sets · **Length:** 2:30
**Logline:** A poor Brahmin walks for days to see his childhood friend, now King of Dwarka, carrying only a cloth bundle of flattened rice and too ashamed to offer it. The King snatches it with joy, eats a handful, and Sudama walks home empty-handed, only to find his hut turned into a palace.
**Why it works:** friendship plus devotion, a story every Indian family knows, and Krishna-Sudama is beloved across regions. It's very shareable on Janmashtami and Friendship Day.
**Care:** the Krishna design must be reverent and beautiful, and the palace set is big. Do it after you have finished 2–3 simpler films.

## 5. Aakhri Ball (Last Ball)
**Type:** cricket underdog · **Build:** 4–6 kids (one base rig with variations), 1S (a gully with a "glass todi to out" wall) · **Length:** 2:00
**Logline:** The gully boys let Munni play only as a fielder, and only because she owns the ball. Last over, six runs to win, and their bowler hurts his wrist. Munni bowls the final ball with the spin she has practised alone against a wall for months.
**Why it works:** cricket, gully rules ("one tip one hand") and girl-power. **Hard:** several kids on screen at once.

## 6. Report Card
**Type:** relatable comedy · **Build:** 3C (kid, mummy, papa), 1S · **Length:** 60s
**Logline:** A kid walks home with a bad report card and imagines everything Papa will do (the slow-motion flying chappal, the "Sharma ji ka beta" speech). Papa opens it, looks at the marks, and quietly says "मेरे भी इतने ही आते थे" (I got the same marks).
**Why it works:** "this is my house" relatability, which gets people tagging their siblings. It's easy to make sequels.

## 7. Gamosa
**Type:** Assam heritage, emotional · **Build:** 2C (reuses Dadi as Aita), 2S (loom house, city hostel) · **Length:** 2:00
**Logline:** Aita weaves a gamosa on her loom for her grandson, who is leaving for a city college. He shoves it into his bag, embarrassed. On a lonely Bihu night in a hostel far from home, he wraps it around his neck, and the red border smells of home.
**Why it works:** strong Assamese pride and the "away from home" feeling for migrants from every state. It's the best idea for your Assam audience.

## 8. Rhino Calf in the Flood
**Type:** Assam nature · **Build:** a baby rhino (quadruped rig, hard) + a boatman, 1S (flooded grassland) · **Length:** 2:00
**Logline:** During the monsoon floods at Kaziranga, a rhino calf is separated from its mother. A boatman and his daughter guide it to the highlands.
**Why it works:** cute animals plus Assam, a real situation (flood rescues), and wildlife lovers across India. **Hard:** a four-legged rig and water simulation.

## 9. Bandar aur Magarmachh, retold
**Type:** Panchatantra moral · **Build:** 2 animals, 1S (river and jamun tree) · **Length:** 1:30
**Logline:** The classic tale of the clever monkey who escapes the crocodile by claiming he left his heart in the jamun tree, told with modern comic timing.
**Why it works:** it's public domain, already loved, and good for kids' channels. **Weakness:** many channels already do it, so it needs a fresh visual style.

## 10. Rakhi on the Border
**Type:** patriotic + sibling · **Build:** 2C, 3S (village, post office, snowy army post) · **Length:** 2:00
**Logline:** A girl's rakhi travels by bus, train, mule and helicopter to her brother at a snowbound army post, arriving exactly on Raksha Bandhan morning.
**Why it works:** huge emotion on two big dates (Raksha Bandhan, Independence Day). **Hard:** many locations. Show the army respectfully and keep details accurate ⚠.

## 11. Chalak Chhotu aur Lalchi Seth (Clever Chhotu and the Greedy Moneylender)
**Type:** folk wit (Birbal / Tenali Raman style, original story) · **Build:** 2–3C, 1S (village mela) · **Length:** 1:30
**Logline:** A greedy moneylender cheats a village kid at the fair, and the kid uses the moneylender's own greed to win back his money.
**Why it works:** audiences love clever-trick stories. It reuses Chhotu.

## 12. Patang (The Kite)
**Type:** festival underdog · **Build:** 2–3C, 1S (rooftops) · **Length:** 1:30
**Logline:** On Makar Sankranti a boy flies a patched-up kite glued with rice paste against the rich kid's shiny new one, and the patched kite wins the sky.
**Timing:** Makar Sankranti / Magh Bihu is mid-January 2027, about 11 weeks away, which is tight but possible if it's your second film.

---

## Suggested order
1. **Khanak** (evergreen). Build Chhotu, Dadi and the home set. Learn the whole pipeline once.
2. **Bhootu** ep. 1–3 (60–90s each). Reuse Dadi and the home. Fast output keeps the channel active.
3. **Tedha Diya** for Diwali 2027, **Gamosa** for Bihu, **Sudama** once you're confident.
