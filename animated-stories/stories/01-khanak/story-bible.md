# खनक · KHANAK (The Clink): story bible
**Type:** emotional family · **Length:** 2:18 · **Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 24 fps · **Dialogue:** 14 short lines (Hindi; English subs; Assamese dub)
**Cast:** Chhotu, Dadi, Gupta-ji (one shot) · **Sets:** home room, bazaar street with two shops and the optician counter, school gate

## 1. Logline
An 8-year-old saves every coin in his clay gullak for a red cricket bat, while his grandmother squints through cracked, taped glasses and keeps saying "चल जाएगा" (it'll do). When he finally smashes the gullak to buy her new glasses instead, he finds far more coins than he ever saved, and a shaky note in her handwriting: "छोटू के बैट के लिए" (for Chhotu's bat).

## 2. Theme
**In Indian families, "चल जाएगा" is how love hides itself.**
Elders say "it'll do" so the children can have more. The film ends when the child says it back. That line is the whole film; protect it.

## 3. Why Indian viewers will feel it
- **The gullak.** Almost every Indian adult had one and remembers the day they broke it.
- **"चल जाएगा".** Everyone has a dadi, nani, maa or papa who said it about their own glasses, slippers or phone.
- **The pallu knot full of coins.** A grandmother's secret purse.
- **Both sacrifice for each other.** The reveal makes viewers cry, and the last line makes them smile.
- **The ending asks viewers to tag the person who always says "chal jayega" for them.** That's built for shares.

## 4. Characters

### Chhotu (8): the saver
- **Want:** the shiny red-handled bat in Sharma Sports' window (₹500).
- **Need:** to see that Dadi has been quietly going without, for him.
- **Personality:** cricket-mad and cheerful. He mimes cover drives at every reflection and counts his coins by the sound of the rattle. He's kind without thinking about it: he threads Dadi's needle without even looking up.
- **Look:** 3.5 heads tall, big round head, big eyes, a gap in his front teeth (shows in every grin), a slightly too-big faded sky-blue school shirt, navy shorts, a black thread on his wrist, rubber chappals at home. Hair: a chunky stylised mop (sculpted mesh, no particle hair).
- **Prop he's never without:** a "bat" made from a wooden plank wrapped with old cycle-tube rubber at the handle.
- **Key expressions:** dreamy (at the window), proud (chalk progress), puzzled (heavy gullak), guilty (Dadi trips over his plank), resolved (the hammer), welling up (the note), cheeky grin ("चल जाएगा").

### Dadi, Kamla Devi (about 70): the hider
- **Want:** Chhotu gets his bat.
- **Need:** to let someone take care of her for once.
- **Personality:** warm and funny, and she laughs at herself. She never complains, and "चल जाएगा" is her answer to everything. She knows every corner of the house by feel, which is how she reaches the gullak in the dark.
- **Look:** 4.5 heads tall, small and round, with a soft bun of grey hair. She wears an off-white cotton saree with a thin maroon border and glass bangles (their *chhan-chhan* is her sound signature). The **pallu end is knotted** around a few coins. Her spectacles have a brown frame, the left lens is cracked, and the bridge is wrapped in white tape.
- **Key expressions:** squinting, laughing at her own mistake, brushing it off ("चल जाएगा"), tear-filled wonder (seeing clearly), mock-scolding ("पगले…").
- **Her blurry POV:** each time we see through her eyes, the image is soft. It snaps into focus only once, when the new glasses go on.

### Gupta-ji (about 55): the optician (one shot)
- A kind, moustached small-town optician with half-moon reading glasses and a pen behind his ear. He remembers that Dadi got her eyes tested months ago and never bought the glasses. He says one line.

### Off-screen
- **Maa and Papa** work long hours, so they are never on screen, which keeps the cast to two. Show them only through props: Papa's helmet on a nail, Maa's dupatta on the clothes line, a third pair of slippers at the door.
- **Dadaji** (late): a framed photo with a sandalwood garland on the wall.

## 5. World
A small town that could be anywhere in India. Keep the details specific, but not tied to one state.

**Home (one room plus a kitchen corner)**
- Lime-washed turquoise walls (#5FB3A8), a red oxide floor (#8B3A2B), and a window with an iron grill and a wide sill where the **gullak** sits.
- A charpai with a cotton durrie, a steel almirah, a ceiling fan, a wall calendar, a **zero-watt bulb** (the night glow), and the Dadaji photo.
- The kitchen corner has a two-burner gas stove with a red LPG cylinder, steel jars with masking-tape labels "नमक" (salt) and "चीनी" (sugar), and steel tumblers.
- A **chalk drawing of a bat** on the wall next to the window, with "₹500" at the top. Chhotu fills it in from the bottom as he saves, like a progress bar.

**Bazaar street**
- Two shops side by side with hand-painted signboards: **SHARMA SPORTS / शर्मा स्पोर्ट्स** (with the red bat on a stand in the window) and **GUPTA OPTICALS / गुप्ता ऑप्टिकल्स** (with spectacles on a rotating stand). Tangled overhead wires, a shutter half-up, a dog asleep in the shade.
- Optician's counter: a glass-top counter, an eye-test chart on the wall, and a thick, old register.

**School gate**
- A green gate and a **barf ka gola** cart with bright syrup bottles and a cardboard sign "गोला ₹10". No vendor on screen, just a hand.

## 7. Sound motifs
Sound tells this story as much as pictures do.
- **Clink:** a coin into the gullak. One crisp clink is Chhotu.
- **Bangles (chhan-chhan):** Dadi. In the night-mystery scene, the faint bangle sound is the clue to who adds the coins. Sharp viewers will catch it, and they'll say so in the comments.
- **Rattle:** shaking the gullak. A thin rattle early on, then a fat, heavy one.
- **Crack:** her glasses breaking. The music cuts to silence.
- **Two clinks:** the final shot. Dadi's coin, then Chhotu's.

## 9. Story logic checks (so the comments can't nitpick)
- **How did he know her lens power?** Gupta-ji tested her eyes months ago. She never bought the glasses ("चल जाएगा"), and his register has her number. This is shown visually in shot S25.
- **How did she find the gullak in the dark?** It sits on the same windowsill she has walked past for years, and the night shot shows her bangles only, not her eyes.
- **Why is the note's handwriting crooked?** She wrote it without being able to see. Make the letters uneven and sloping off the line, a small, heartbreaking detail.
- **Money:** Chhotu saved about ₹300 and Dadi added about ₹200, which makes ₹500, exactly the price of the bat. He spends it on glasses. (The optician's price is never shown, so nobody can argue about it.)
- **Is Dadi hurt?** No. She stumbles and sits down hard, then laughs. Nobody gets injured; the only thing that breaks is the glasses.
