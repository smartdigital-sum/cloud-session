## ✅ Verified (no change needed)
**Video 1**
- Louis XVI 1774, age 20; married Marie Antoinette (Austria); empty treasury from long wars
- Helped 13 American colonies vs Britain; war added 1+ billion livres to a debt of 2+ billion; lenders charged 10% interest
- Expenses: army, court, government offices, universities, so taxes went up
- Three estates; peasants ~90% of the population, few owned land; ~60% of land held by nobles, Church and rich third estate
- First two estates exempt from taxes by birth; feudal services (house, fields, army, roads); tithe to the Church; taille plus indirect taxes on salt and tobacco paid only by the third estate
- Population 23 million (1715) → 28 million (1789); bread prices rose, wages didn't; subsistence crisis definition
- Middle class wealth from overseas trade and woollen/silk textiles; lawyers and officials; merit, not birth
- Locke (*Two Treatises*, against the divine and absolute right of the monarch), Rousseau (*Social Contract*), Montesquieu (*Spirit of the Laws*, three-way separation; adopted in the USA)
- Ideas spread through salons, coffee-houses, books and newspapers, read aloud to people who couldn't read
- Estates General last met in 1614; 5 May 1789; 300 + 300 seated, 600 standing at the back; peasants, artisans and women kept out; ~40,000 letters
- One vote per estate; the third estate demanded one vote per member (from Rousseau); the king refused; walkout
- 20 June, indoor tennis court, National Assembly; oath; Mirabeau (noble family), Abbé Sieyès (former priest, *What is the Third Estate?*)
- Severe winter, bad harvest, bakers hoarding, women storming shops; troops ordered into Paris; Bastille on 14 July, a symbol of despotic power
- Rumours of brigands; peasants attacked chateaux, looted grain, burnt records of dues; nobles fled
- The king recognised the Assembly; 4 August decree: feudal dues, clergy privileges and tithes abolished, Church lands taken; at least 2 billion livres

**Video 2**
- Constitution of 1791 limited the king; separation into legislature, executive and judiciary; indirect elections (citizens → electors → Assembly)
- Active citizen: men 25+, tax equal to at least 3 days' wages; all others passive
- Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen: life, speech, opinion, equality before law; "natural and inalienable"
- Symbols: broken chain, fasces, red cap, eye in triangle, snake ring, winged woman, blue-white-red
- Secret talks with the King of Prussia; war on Prussia and Austria in April 1792; volunteers; Marseillaise by Roget de L'Isle, sung first by Marseilles volunteers, now the national anthem
- Constitution of 1791 gave rights only to the rich; political clubs; Jacobins led by Robespierre; members' backgrounds; sans-culottes (striped trousers vs knee breeches)
- 10 August 1792, Tuileries; guards killed, king held hostage; royal family imprisoned; all men 21+ could vote
- Convention; 21 Sept 1792 republic; republic definition; Louis executed 21 Jan 1793 at Place de la Concorde; Marie Antoinette soon after
- Reign of Terror 1793–94; "enemies" tried by a revolutionary tribunal; guillotine (two poles and a blade, Dr Guillotin)
- Wage and price ceilings, rationing, grain at fixed prices, white flour banned, equality bread; Citoyen/Citoyenne; churches turned into barracks or offices; Robespierre guillotined in July 1794
- Directory: wealthy middle class; property qualification; two councils → five-member Directory; clashes → Napoleon
- Women: jobs, lower wages, little education; ~60 clubs; Society of Revolutionary and Republican Women; demand for equal political rights; laws on schooling, marriage, divorce and jobs; Olympe de Gouges 1791; clubs closed during the Terror; Olympe executed 1793; vote in 1946
- Slave trade from the 17th century, Bordeaux and Nantes, 3-month voyage, plantation owners; sugar, coffee, indigo; ports grew rich; Convention freed slaves in 1794; Napoleon brought slavery back 10 years later; abolished in 1848
- Censorship abolished after the Bastille; print spread to villages; plays, songs and festivals
- Napoleon emperor in 1804; private property law; decimal weights and measures; Waterloo 1815; legacy of liberty and democratic rights; Tipu Sultan and Rammohan Roy

## ❌ Fixes needed

### V1 · Scene 001 (hook): who stormed the Bastille
The script says thousands of people went looking for weapons and stormed the Bastille. NCERT says **about 7,000** gathered at the town hall and broke into buildings looking for arms, and then **a group of several hundred** marched to and stormed the Bastille.
Suggested VO:
> चौदह जुलाई, सत्रह सौ नवासी। पेरिस में करीब सात हज़ार लोग जमा हुए और हथियार ढूँढने लगे… फिर कुछ सौ लोगों ने राजा की सबसे बदनाम जेल — बास्तील — पर धावा बोल दिया।

### V1 · Scene 007: why the treasury was empty
NCERT gives **two** reasons: long years of war **and** the cost of the extravagant court at Versailles. The script has only the wars.
Suggested VO:
> पर राजा को विरासत में मिला एक खाली खज़ाना। लंबे युद्धों ने फ्रांस का पैसा खत्म कर दिया था, और ऊपर से वर्साय के आलीशान दरबार का भारी खर्च।

### Nice-to-have (in NCERT, optional)
- V1 S009/S011: to be an elector or Assembly member, a man had to be in the **highest tax bracket**.
- V2 S019: while men fought at the front, women had to earn a living and run families.
- V2 S020: Jacobins were named after the former **convent of St Jacob** in Paris.
- V2 S021: sans-culottes men wore the red cap of liberty, but **women weren't allowed to**. This sets up Part 5.
- V2 S040: the ideals of freedom, equality and fraternity kept inspiring Europe through the next century.
- V2 S046: divorce could be applied for **by both women and men**.
- V2 S047: Olympe addressed her declaration to the **Queen and the National Assembly**; in 1793 she criticised the Jacobins for closing women's clubs and was charged with **treason**.
